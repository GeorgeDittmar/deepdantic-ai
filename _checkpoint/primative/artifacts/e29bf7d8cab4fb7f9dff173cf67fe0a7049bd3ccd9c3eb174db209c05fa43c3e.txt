The stars outside the observation window of the ISSV *Peregrine* burned like scattered diamonds against the infinite black. Captain Elara Voss pressed her palm against the cold transparisteel, her breath fogging a small circle on the surface. Behind her, the ship hummed—a steady, comforting vibration that had been the only sound in her life for eleven years.

They had been traveling for eleven years, eleven months, and three days toward the Kepler-442 system. Eleven years since Earth's last transmission. Eleven years since the Silence began.

"Approaching the nebula boundary, Captain," said Kael, the ship's navigator, his fingers dancing across the holographic controls. "Sensors are picking up unusual energy signatures. I've never seen anything like it."

Elara turned from the window. The *Peregrine* was an old ship—retrofitted, patched, and stubbornly alive. She loved her like family. "Show me."

The main display shifted, painting the void in shades of violet and gold. The nebula loomed ahead like a cosmic cathedral, its tendrils of gas and dust twisting in slow, hypnotic patterns. But within its heart, something pulsed—a rhythm of light that seemed almost deliberate.

"Is that... a signal?" whispered Dr. Tessa Okoro, the ship's xenobiologist, appearing at Elara's shoulder.

"Could be," Elara said. "Or it could be a trick of the light. Our instruments have been glitching since we passed Jupiter."

"Since the radiation storms," Kael added quietly. He still hadn't fully recovered from the incident, though he'd never admit it. The storms had scrambled their long-range communications array and forced them to improvise. Improvisation was the *Peregrine*'s specialty.

Elara made her decision. "Course correction. Take us into the nebula. Maximum sensor sweep. Tessa, prepare the sample collection array."

"Into it?" Kael's voice cracked slightly. "Captain, we don't know what's inside—"

"We don't know what's out here either, Kael. That's why we're here."

The *Peregrine* pushed forward, slipping into the nebula's embrace. The ship shuddered as charged particles lashed against the hull. Lights flickered. Alarms chimed, then settled. Through the window, the nebula unfolded like a living thing—swirling clouds of ionized gas, stellar nurseries where newborn stars ignited in brilliant blue fury, and shadows that moved with purpose.

Then they saw it.

At the center of the nebula, suspended in a cradle of luminous gas, was a structure. It was vast—easily hundreds of kilometers across—geometric and ancient, its surface etched with patterns that seemed to shift and rearrange themselves when viewed directly. It hummed with the same energy signature the sensors had detected.

"Artificial," Tessa breathed. "It has to be."

"Or it was," Kael murmured. "Look at the decay on the lower sections. That thing has been sitting here for millennia."

Elara felt a thrill she hadn't experienced since she was a child reading old space exploration logs. "Record everything. Every scan, every image, every data point. We're the first."

The *Peregrine* circled the structure slowly, its instruments drinking in the information. Tessa cataloged organic compounds in the surrounding gas that shouldn't exist in a nebula. Kael mapped the geometry of the structure and found mathematical patterns that echoed prime number sequences. Elara simply watched, her heart pounding.

"Captain," Kael said after a long moment. "I'm detecting a power source. It's... it's still active."

Elara straightened. "Can we interact with it?"

"I think so. But Captain—this changes everything. We're not just exploring anymore. We're about to make first contact."

Elara smiled, looking out at the ancient structure that had waited in the darkness for an unknown length of time, and by extension, for someone like her.

"Then let's say hello," she said.
